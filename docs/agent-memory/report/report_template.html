Fly Colony · Phase 2 · spec 005 M7–M10 · 20–21 Oct 2026

Phase 2 gate run

The embodied fly ran its first full set of emergence gates: a real connectome brain (FAFB 783, about 139,000 neurons) closed in a loop with senses, a body and a world. Every trial started from world stimuli only. No neuron was stimulated, and nothing was tuned per gate. This page reports every result, including the failures, and what caused them.

Summary

What passed, what failed, and why

The machinery works. The loop runs 2.1–2.3× faster with bit-identical results. Driving each mapped descending-neuron group directly produces its movement in 20 of 20 flies for all 8 groups. With the motor output clamped, nothing moves in 50 of 50 trials.

The behaviors do not emerge yet. Hungry flies on sugar extend the proboscis about four times more than controls, but rarely far enough to drink. Bitter cuts that extension by about 70%, but the feeding criterion cannot register it. In the plume, flies barely walk at all. At night under sleep pressure flies go almost still, but brief twitches break every bout before it counts as sleep.

One global cause dominates. The production brain's background noise keeps inhibitory interneurons tonically active. Switching it off restores the sugar response of the motor neuron MN9 from 22 Hz to 82 Hz (reference model: 78 Hz). A second cause is in the taste layer: a sucrose drop drives only 9 of the 20 sugar neurons the reference model uses.

Diagnosis

Why sugar does not reach the proboscis

The Phase-1 sugar assay drives the 20 sugar neurons of Shiu et al. (2024) at 150 Hz and reads MN9, the proboscis-extension motor neuron. The production model differs from the validated reference model in four global ways. Each was switched off in turn (8 flies per configuration).

Background noise alone moves MN9 by 55–60 Hz in either direction. Graded optic-lobe units and neuromodulation change nothing; the coarser 0.5 ms timestep costs a little.

The taste layer drives fewer sugar neurons

The closed-loop sugar channel maps receptor neurons by their FlyWire sub-class. Of the 20 sugar neurons the reference model drives, only the 9 LB3c cells are in it. A sucrose drop also makes them fire at about 35–70 Hz after adaptation, not 150 Hz.

LB3b is annotated as sensing both sugar and low salt, so it should respond to sucrose. LB4b and LB3d are a disagreement between annotation sources rather than a clear bug.

Sleep is fragmented by twitches

An exploratory check of the sleep protocol (2 flies per arm, 15 s) separates the arms clearly, even though the gate scored zero for both.

Most twitches are small steering movements. The steering threshold sits at the 99th percentile of resting activity, so noise crosses it about 1% of the time, which matches what is seen here.

The same inhibition shows up in the open-loop test

Holding each group at a multiple of its spike threshold, most groups respond at 2×. The backward-walking neurons (MDN) need 4× and the Giant Fiber, which triggers escape, fires only at 8×, because the resting network inhibits them. That matters for the looming gate once it can run.

  • Senses went from about 2,250 GPU operations per step to about 100.
  • The brain's optic-lobe coupling update was filling the 20 GB card at 32 flies; it is now chunked and bounded.
  • What remains is launch overhead (about 10 µs per small GPU operation on this driver) and memory traffic. Fused kernels and CUDA graphs are planned for Phase 9.

Motor threshold calibration (M8, commit b47140c)

Each movement's threshold was set to the 99th percentile of its group's firing in resting flies, a rule fixed before looking at the data. Under the old thresholds a resting fly was moving of the time.

Next

Options from here

  1. Re-calibrate background noise globally against a published target for spontaneous firing in the fly central brain. The current value was tuned to a 12 Hz mean. This changes the brain model, so the Phase-1 timestep gate and every Phase-2 gate must be re-run.
  2. Fix the LB3b taste mapping so cells annotated sugar/low_salt respond to sucrose, with the annotation as the citation.
  3. Re-run the M8 calibration and all gates on the corrected model, about 11 GPU-hours.
  4. Unblock the looming gate by downloading the FlyWire "Visual Neuron Columns" file into data/raw/flywire_columns/.
  5. If a fix is not justified by the literature, accept a partial repertoire and report these results as final, as the project plan allows (§17).

Not allowed by the project rules (CONST-7.3): lowering a threshold for one gate, or changing a pass criterion after seeing the data.
